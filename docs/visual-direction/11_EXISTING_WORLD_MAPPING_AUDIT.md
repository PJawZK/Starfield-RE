# Existing-World Mapping Audit

Status: provisional mapping pass

## Purpose

The six reference-world concepts are now treated primarily as **overhaul archetypes for existing Starfield worlds**, not as six new planets that must each be authored from zero.

Selection principle:

> Choose the vanilla world that already satisfies the greatest number of physical, ecological, atmospheric, settlement, and lore requirements, then spend effort transforming identity rather than recreating basic planetary context.

The internal archetype names may remain useful during design, but the released overhaul should generally retain the existing Starfield world names unless there is a strong lore reason not to.

## Provisional lock-ins

### Eden-9 -> Jemison

Status: strong provisional lock.

Why:

- major temperate life-bearing world;
- existing long-term civilian and governmental settlement;
- New Atlantis already makes it the best place to test mature future-human architecture;
- existing farms and secondary sites give the overhaul existing narrative and functional anchors;
- the archetype's purpose is familiarity-by-design, which fits Jemison better than inventing another Earth-like world.

Primary overhaul work:

- stronger ecological coherence;
- more terrain-integrated and ecologically intelligent New Atlantis expansion;
- mature future-human infrastructure rather than contemporary city logic;
- richer wilderness continuity and regional identity.

### Thalassa -> Volii Alpha

Status: strong provisional lock.

Why:

- vanilla world is already 100% ocean;
- temperate, high-O2 atmosphere;
- very strong wind potential;
- Neon already gives the planet a major settlement and extensive existing story content;
- the overhaul can make the *planet surrounding Neon* finally matter rather than creating a second ocean-world setting from scratch.

Primary overhaul work:

- vertical volcanic/tectonic ocean landforms or artificial geological anchors where compatible;
- storm systems and severe marine weather;
- cliff/platform/caldera-style ship routes;
- better integration of Neon with the planetary ocean environment.

## Khepri archetype — airless crater/fracture moon

Target requirements:

- no atmosphere;
- low gravity;
- crater-dominated terrain;
- strong light/shadow contrast;
- minimal existing ecology to preserve;
- enough design freedom for a localized anomaly scar and Crater Run prototype.

### Candidate 1 — Bel I-a

Current fit:

- barren moon;
- atmosphere: none;
- gravity: 0.11 g;
- temperature: temperate;
- biomes: 100% craters;
- very long day: 59 days.

Advantages:

- extremely clean physical fit;
- very low gravity supports dramatic ship-scale terrain;
- long day supports prolonged hard-light/shadow conditions;
- little existing content appears to constrain redesign.

Required changes:

- add the major crater/fracture hierarchy;
- add localized anomaly geology;
- add selected human occupation and Crater Run content.

Assessment: **best current Khepri candidate**.

### Candidate 2 — Fermi III-a

Current fit:

- barren moon;
- atmosphere: none;
- gravity: 0.17 g;
- temperature: temperate;
- biomes: 100% craters;
- Bolide Bombardment trait.

Advantages:

- strong barren baseline;
- low gravity;
- existing impact-related trait complements Khepri's geological history.

Required changes:

- anomaly layer;
- stronger large-scale fracture/collapse terrain;
- authored occupation.

Assessment: **strong alternate**, especially if the existing bombardment trait proves visually useful.

### Candidate 3 — Volii Gamma

Current fit:

- barren moon;
- atmosphere: none;
- gravity: 0.07 g;
- temperature: cold;
- 100% craters;
- Bolide Bombardment trait.

Advantages:

- exceptionally low gravity;
- physically clean moon baseline;
- same system as Volii Alpha could create useful contrast.

Disadvantages:

- using two major archetypes in one system may concentrate overhaul effort too heavily around Volii.

Assessment: **good technical fit, weaker portfolio fit**.

### Candidate 4 — Marduk III-a

Current fit:

- barren moon;
- atmosphere: none;
- gravity: 0.11 g;
- 100% craters;
- Boiled Seas and Bolide Bombardment traits.

Assessment: **usable**, but the vanilla traits point toward a somewhat different geological history than Khepri's clean impact/collapse/anomaly concept.

## Pelagos archetype — dense-atmosphere alien biosphere

Target requirements:

- substantial atmosphere;
- strong atmospheric circulation;
- abundant biological potential;
- water cycle or biological water;
- enough fauna/flora infrastructure to reuse while replacing Earth-like visual grammar;
- ideally existing aeriform or amphibious traits.

### Candidate 1 — Newton II

Current fit:

- gravity: 1.48 g;
- atmosphere: High M;
- water: Biological;
- strong wind potential;
- biomes include rocky desert, ocean, mountains, swamp and frozen plains;
- traits: Aeriform Life, Amphibious Foothold, Ecological Consortium;
- fauna: 8;
- flora: 5.

Advantages:

- unusually close conceptual match before any overhaul work;
- existing Aeriform Life directly supports Pelagos's suspended/atmospheric ecology;
- Amphibious Foothold and Biological water support the saturated lower layer;
- strong gravity gives us an interesting constraint: Pelagos's buoyant/suspended biology must genuinely exploit atmospheric density rather than simply relying on low gravity.

Required changes:

- reorganize biomes into a coherent atmospheric ecology;
- replace ordinary forest/swamp visual logic with horizontal, membrane, tether and filter structures;
- create stronger fog/cloud-layer traversal;
- add world-specific settlements and atmospheric-navigation systems.

Assessment: **excellent fit and current leader**.

### Candidate 2 — Shoza III

Current fit:

- gravity: 1.25 g;
- atmosphere: High O2;
- water: Safe;
- strong wind potential;
- traits: Ecological Consortium, Psychotropic Biota;
- fauna: 10;
- flora: 8;
- large ocean component plus swamp/wetland/savanna regions.

Advantages:

- extremely rich biosphere;
- dense-enough atmosphere category for strong weather and atmospheric ecology;
- abundant existing biological content to reinterpret.

Disadvantages:

- vanilla reads more like a lush Earth-like biosphere, so transformation burden may be higher than Newton II.

Assessment: **very strong alternate**.

### Candidate 3 — Bannoc II

Current fit:

- gravity: 0.91 g;
- atmosphere: Std M;
- water: Biological;
- mixed ocean/forest/savanna/mountain/volcanic biomes;
- traits include Aeriform Life and possible Gravitational Anomaly.

Advantages:

- Aeriform Life provides a direct hook;
- lower gravity supports suspended biology;
- optional anomaly can create localized strange atmospheric behavior.

Disadvantages:

- atmosphere is less naturally suited to the dense-atmosphere Pelagos concept.

Assessment: **good alternate if lower gravity proves more valuable than atmospheric density**.

### Candidate 4 — Cassiopeia I

Current fit:

- temperate;
- Std O2 atmosphere;
- safe water;
- 50% ocean;
- traits: Aeriform Life, Amphibious Foothold, Psychotropic Biota;
- fauna: 7;
- flora: 5.

Assessment: **excellent ecological hooks, weaker atmospheric fit**.

## Vitria archetype — dry mineral storm world

Target requirements:

- substantial hostile atmosphere;
- strong winds;
- little or no surface water;
- dry rocky/desert baseline;
- geology compatible with long-lived directional erosion;
- minimal dense biosphere;
- preferably tectonic/lithosphere traits.

### Candidate 1 — Procyon II

Current fit:

- gravity: 0.96 g;
- temperature: Inferno;
- atmosphere: Extr CO2;
- water: none;
- wind potential: very high;
- biomes: 85% rocky desert, 15% hills;
- traits: Primed For Life, Prismatic Plumes, Turbulent Lithosphere;
- no fauna or flora.

Advantages:

- almost ideal atmospheric and terrain baseline for severe silica/mineral storms;
- high wind potential directly supports the storm gameplay concept;
- Turbulent Lithosphere and Prismatic Plumes provide geological hooks;
- lack of biosphere reduces conflicts with a largely abiotic world.

Required changes:

- cool/reframe local survivability if necessary rather than making the whole concept comfortable;
- author aligned yardang/blade geology;
- implement three-phase storm behavior and terrain shelter logic;
- add adapted human mining/processing infrastructure.

Assessment: **best current Vitria candidate**.

### Candidate 2 — Marduk II

Current fit:

- gravity: 1.11 g;
- temperature: Inferno;
- atmosphere: Extr CO2;
- water: none;
- very high wind potential;
- mostly rocky desert/plateau;
- traits: Charred Ecosystem, Prismatic Plumes, Turbulent Lithosphere;
- no fauna/flora.

Assessment: **near-tie with Procyon II**. Stronger 'burned' historical identity, slightly less clean if we want wind erosion rather than thermal devastation to dominate.

### Candidate 3 — Bondar

Current fit:

- gravity: 1.84 g;
- Inferno;
- Extr CO2;
- no water;
- very high wind;
- rocky/sandy/volcanic terrain;
- Emerging Tectonics and Turbulent Lithosphere.

Advantages:

- excellent hostile-atmosphere/geology fit.

Disadvantages:

- 1.84 g significantly changes traversal and ship-feel assumptions;
- more overt volcanic identity than Vitria currently calls for.

Assessment: **interesting high-gravity variant, not first choice**.

### Candidate 4 — Xi Ophiuchi III

Current fit:

- gravity: 0.77 g;
- Inferno;
- Extr CO2;
- no water;
- very high wind;
- sandy desert/hills/plateau;
- Coralline Landmass and Sonorous Lithosphere.

Assessment: **good physical fit**, though the existing traits do less work for the specific directional-erosion identity.

## Nadir-Tau archetype — permanent directional climate world

Target requirements:

- ideally tidally locked or close enough that permanent/near-permanent stellar geometry can be justified;
- substantial atmosphere but limited enough heat redistribution to preserve strong day/night contrast;
- dry or semi-dry baseline preferred;
- strong wind/circulation potential;
- sparse existing life reduces conflict with a narrow habitability band.

Important caveat:

Current public Starfield planet databases expose day length, atmosphere, temperature, water and traits, but do not provide a reliable explicit 'tidally locked' flag. Therefore this mapping cannot yet be locked from public metadata alone. Rotation/orbit records need to be inspected directly in game data before committing.

### Candidate 1 — Katydid III

Current fit:

- gravity: 1.14 g;
- temperature: Frozen;
- atmosphere: Std N2;
- water: Chemical;
- day length: 100 days;
- biomes: hills, plateau, rocky desert, frozen plains;
- traits: Energetic Rifting, Turbulent Lithosphere;
- no fauna/flora.

Advantages:

- exceptionally long existing day strongly supports a world visually dominated by long-lived stellar direction;
- dry, sparsely biological baseline;
- terrain already spans rocky and frozen forms;
- enough atmosphere for circulation without committing to a lush biosphere.

Required validation:

- inspect actual rotation/orbital relationship;
- determine whether true locking can be achieved safely without breaking sky/time systems;
- determine whether regional climate bands can be authored or convincingly simulated.

Assessment: **best provisional Nadir-Tau candidate**, pending record-level verification.

### Candidate 2 — Bel II-a

Current fit:

- gravity: 0.35 g;
- Cold;
- Thin CO2;
- no water;
- day length: 94 hours;
- sandy desert, hills, rocky desert, mountains, frozen plains;
- traits: Crystalline Crust and Turbulent Lithosphere.

Advantages:

- mixed warm-looking rock/frozen terrain provides good visual raw material;
- thin atmosphere and low gravity could create a harsher, narrower twilight habitability model.

Disadvantages:

- day is long but nowhere near Katydid III's 100-day cycle;
- low gravity moves it away from the terrestrial-world feel originally intended.

Assessment: **good alternate if a harsher low-gravity terminator world becomes preferable**.

### Candidate 3 — Bel IV-c

Current fit:

- gravity: 0.48 g;
- Temperate;
- Thin M corrosive atmosphere;
- safe water;
- day length: 73 hours;
- ocean/swamp/frozen plains;
- Aeriform Life, Emerging Tectonics and Global Glacial Recession traits.

Advantages:

- environmental gradients already exist conceptually;
- atmosphere/ecological traits could support unusual twilight weather.

Disadvantages:

- far wetter than the current Nadir-Tau design;
- would require more fundamental ecological rewriting.

Assessment: **interesting but higher-work option**.

### Candidate 4 — Katydid II

Current fit:

- gravity: 0.91 g;
- Frozen;
- Std M atmosphere;
- safe water;
- rocky desert/mountains/frozen volcanic terrain;
- Energetic Rifting, Primed For Life, Turbulent Lithosphere.

Assessment: **good physical world, but no obvious rotational advantage from public metadata**.

## Current mapping recommendation

| Archetype | Existing Starfield world | Confidence | Reason |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Eden-9 | Jemison | High | existing mature Earth-like UC world and New Atlantis |
| Thalassa | Volii Alpha | High | existing 100% ocean planet and Neon |
| Khepri | Bel I-a | Medium-high | extremely clean low-g airless crater moon |
| Pelagos | Newton II | High | High-M atmosphere + Aeriform Life + Amphibious Foothold + rich biosphere |
| Vitria | Procyon II | High | Extr CO2, no water, high wind, rocky desert, turbulent lithosphere |
| Nadir-Tau | Katydid III | Medium/conditional | 100-day rotation and dry/frozen atmospheric baseline; tidal-lock state must be verified in records |

## Why these mappings are attractive as a set

They distribute the overhaul across multiple systems rather than concentrating all flagship concepts together.

They also preserve contrast:

- Jemison remains the familiar mature biosphere;
- Volii Alpha becomes the ocean/storm benchmark;
- Bel I-a becomes the airless flight/geology benchmark;
- Newton II becomes the strongly alien ecology benchmark;
- Procyon II becomes the atmospheric/geological storm benchmark;
- Katydid III becomes the planetary-climate-geometry benchmark if technical validation succeeds.

## Next verification work

Before declaring the mapping final:

1. inspect Starfield data records for each candidate;
2. identify worldspace/planet/climate/biome records and dependencies;
3. verify major quest or location dependencies that could make transformation dangerous;
4. inspect the rotation/orbit representation for Katydid III and other Nadir-Tau candidates;
5. identify which biome/weather parameters are actually editable through CK/xEdit and which require native work;
6. check whether each candidate has handcrafted locations that must be preserved or deliberately redesigned.

The mappings should only become implementation locks once those record-level checks confirm that the vanilla world's hidden dependencies do not outweigh its visual/physical advantages.
