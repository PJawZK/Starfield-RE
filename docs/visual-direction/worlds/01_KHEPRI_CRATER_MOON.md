# Khepri — Anomaly-Scarred Crater Moon

Status: reference world / flight-first concept

## Purpose

Khepri is the control environment for ship-scale terrain and the planned Crater Run concept. It should prove that an airless body can feel distinctive without relying on vegetation, weather, saturated color, or physically implausible surface liquids.

Khepri should read first as a believable moon and only second as an anomalous Starfield location.

## One-sentence pitch

Khepri is a dead crater moon whose already-broken crust was later scarred by a localized Caelumite-linked gravitational anomaly, leaving collapse canyons, sagged terraces, hidden voids, and embedded human outposts across a basin built for ship-scale exploration.

## Core physical rules

- airless or effectively vacuum surface environment;
- low gravity;
- ancient heavily cratered crust;
- one dominant ancient impact basin provides the primary landscape structure;
- deep impact fracturing and later crustal collapse created large subsurface voids;
- collapsed lava/impact tubes and fracture corridors are common within the main basin;
- extreme direct-light/shadow contrast;
- exposed regolith, impact glass, breccia, metallic inclusions, and localized mineral seams;
- no persistent exposed surface rivers, lakes, or unexplained liquid flows.

### Surface fluid rule

Khepri's surface language is dominated by solids, dust, frost, vapor, and mineral alteration.

Acceptable surface phenomena include:

- frost or volatile deposits in permanently shadowed cold traps;
- transient vapor or sublimation plumes where a volatile-bearing layer is exposed;
- impact or thermal melt only where a credible heat source exists;
- sealed or pressurized subsurface fluids if later exploration requires them;
- very rare transient brine-like behavior only if a specific pressure/temperature/chemistry case supports it.

Bright terrain features must not visually read as rivers of unexplained anomaly fluid. Prefer exposed mineral seams, sintered/glassy regolith, reflective deposits, Caelumite-bearing fracture faces, or frost in shadow.

## Formation history

Khepri's visual identity should encode a layered geological history.

### Stage 1 — ancient impact

A very large impact created:

- the dominant basin;
- concentric terraces;
- ejecta ridges;
- central uplift structures;
- deep radial and circumferential fracturing.

### Stage 2 — void formation and collapse

Later thermal and structural evolution created or enlarged:

- lava/impact tubes;
- buried voids;
- subsidence bowls;
- collapse pits;
- long fracture canyons;
- crater-wall cavities.

### Stage 3 — localized anomaly disturbance

A limited sector of the basin was later affected by a Caelumite-bearing, Artifact-adjacent gravitational anomaly.

The anomaly should not remake the entire moon. Its visible consequences are concentrated around one scarred region where already-damaged crust became unusually unstable.

Potential long-term effects:

- terraces sagging inward more smoothly than ordinary collapse predicts;
- circular or spiral fracture fields superimposed on impact geometry;
- aligned collapse pits;
- displaced strata;
- exposed Caelumite-bearing or highly reflective mineral seams;
- fine debris fields that appear subtly combed or dragged;
- localized dust or particulate behavior that hints at abnormal force fields;
- rare small-object levitation or unstable debris only close to the strongest anomaly pockets.

The visual goal is: **real geology that has been interfered with**, not magical corruption.

## Shape language

Primary form: enormous nested crater rings and basin terraces.

Secondary form: long branching fracture trenches and collapse corridors that cut crater walls.

Anomaly form: sagged rings, offset shelves, smooth subsidence bowls, aligned pits, and displaced fracture geometry.

Detail form: sharp regolith shelves, impact-glass veins, fractured slabs, exposed mineral seams.

Void form: collapsed tubes, shadowed fissures, skylights, crater-wall cavities.

Directional bias: radial impact geometry intersected by branching linear fractures, with localized anomalous curvature in the scarred sector.

## Horizon read

Khepri should read as broad, monumental, and quiet rather than cluttered. One or two giant crater rims dominate the skyline. Fractures create visible cuts through otherwise open regolith expanses.

The anomaly sector should be detectable at long range not because it glows, but because the geometry looks subtly wrong: smoother sagging, repeated collapse rings, exposed bright seams, and unnatural alignment.

The world must remain visually strong in grayscale.

## Palette and light

Restrained materials:

- charcoal rock;
- pale mineral dust;
- dark impact glass;
- metallic inclusions;
- muted reflective Caelumite-bearing seams;
- localized cold frost in permanent shadow.

Identity comes primarily from hard lighting, deep shadow, and geometry.

Low sun angles should create kilometer-scale shadow shapes.

Do not use constant emissive anomaly lighting as the main cue.

## Human adaptation

There are three plausible reasons for human occupation:

- mining exposed rare/mineral-rich seams;
- research into gravitational/anomalous crust behavior;
- covert occupation exploiting terrain masking and hidden voids.

Settlements prefer:

- crater walls for shielding;
- fracture interiors for concealment and structural access;
- subsurface voids for habitation;
- rim positions for sensors and defenses;
- reinforced shelves near stable sections of the anomaly scar.

Architecture should be recessed, buried, cliff-cut, or anchored into existing terrain wherever practical.

Exterior structures should be sparse and functional.

## Signature regions

### Outer Rim Shelf

Broad barren plateau, hard sunlight, long sightlines, sparse installations. Serves as the visual and gameplay arrival zone.

### Terraced Basin Wall

Massive stepped relief with cliffside infrastructure and readable descent routes.

### Fracture Canyons

Long trenches and nested corridors sized to support low-altitude ship traversal and ambush geometry.

### Collapse Field

Pitted terrain, skylights, broken shelves, sink structures, and entrances into subsurface voids.

### Anomaly Scar

The localized region of altered geometry: smoother subsidence basins, offset strata, aligned pits, exposed mineral/Caelumite-bearing seams, subtle particulate oddities, and the strongest research/mining presence.

### Interior Voids

Collapsed tubes and artificial excavations supporting hidden hangars, pirate occupation, industrial spaces, and transitions to on-foot exploration.

## Ship-scale gameplay

Khepri is designed around flight routes:

- diving over crater rims;
- traversing deep radial trenches;
- following fractures to hidden installations;
- low passes beneath overhangs/collapsed shelves;
- navigating around anomaly-deformed terrain;
- entering large artificial/natural cavities where technically feasible.

The terrain should visually suggest routes before the player reaches them.

## Signature location — The Scar

A gigantic fracture-and-collapse region cuts through an older crater terrace. From high altitude it appears mostly geological. At low altitude, displaced strata, aligned sink structures, reflective mineral seams, and instrument anomalies reveal something unusual.

Artificial lights and heat signatures then expose human occupation built into the wall.

Approach sequence:

```text
crater rim
-> terraced descent
-> fracture corridor
-> first anomaly geometry
-> sensor disturbance/contact
-> surface defenses
-> collapse canyon
-> concealed hangar / external landing shelf
```

This becomes the natural environment for the Crater Run prototype.

## Alienness target

Khepri is not biologically alien. Its job is to prove that barren worlds can still be memorable through geology, scale, darkness, plausible material behavior, human adaptation, and a restrained Starfield-lore anomaly layer.

## Hard visual rules

- no persistent unexplained surface liquids;
- no random glowing corruption;
- no generic crystal-field shorthand;
- no evenly distributed rock clutter;
- no mountains placed only to fill horizons;
- no giant human megastructures that visually overpower the basin;
- anomaly effects remain localized and secondary to believable geology;
- large quiet areas are intentional.

## Failure mode to avoid

If the player sees the anomaly sector and first thinks "magic terrain" rather than "something has physically altered this moon," the concept has gone too far.
