# Pelagos — Dense-Atmosphere Float Biosphere

Status: reference world / biosphere concept

## Purpose

Pelagos is the living-world counterpoint to Khepri. It should prove that a richly inhabited planet can feel genuinely alien without defaulting to Earth forests, tropical recolors, mushroom fields, or constant bioluminescence.

The world must derive its visual identity from atmospheric physics, wind loading, buoyancy, ecological structure, and visibility.

## One-sentence pitch

Pelagos is a humid, dense-atmosphere world where persistent winds and strong buoyancy pressures have driven life into broad horizontal forms, suspended colonies, drifting filter-feeders, and layered ecosystems that disappear into haze above unstable saturated ground.

## Physical baseline

Pelagos should be designed from the physical environment outward.

Initial reference assumptions:

- atmosphere substantially denser than Earth's, but still compatible with human operation through suitable protection and local technology;
- moderate gravity;
- high humidity and suspended aerosols;
- frequent cloud, fog, and haze layers;
- strong prevailing winds and vertical circulation;
- recurring electrical storms;
- abundant surface and atmospheric water cycle;
- broad regions of saturated, marsh-like, porous, or structurally weak ground;
- direct sunlight frequently diffused by atmosphere and cloud.

Exact pressure, chemistry, and temperature remain intentionally unfixed until we determine what best fits Starfield's existing planetary parameters. The visual rules should not depend on impossible chemistry.

## Fluid and weather rules

Unlike Khepri, Pelagos can plausibly support persistent surface liquid because its atmosphere provides pressure and the climate is designed around a stable hydrological cycle.

Water is the default working fluid unless later Starfield lore/data suggests a better compatible case.

Acceptable phenomena include:

- standing water and saturated ground;
- broad shallow wetlands;
- heavy rain or mist;
- cloud decks at multiple altitudes;
- condensation on flora and structures;
- airborne droplets and aerosols;
- strong runoff during storm events;
- localized flooded depressions and channels.

Avoid arbitrary exotic liquids purely for visual novelty. If another solvent or chemically unusual liquid is introduced later, its pressure/temperature/chemistry case must be established first.

## Shape language

Primary form: huge horizontal biological canopies and suspended or semi-suspended colonies.

Secondary form: broad fan, ribbon, membrane, sail, and umbrella-like structures shaped by prevailing wind.

Ground form: low, wide, anchored growth that minimizes wind loading.

Detail form: porous tissues, layered aerodynamic surfaces, flexible spars, tensioned membranes, filamentous filter structures.

Void form: fog-filled corridors beneath canopies, gaps between suspended colonies, vertical clear pockets, storm-scoured channels.

Directional bias: horizontal, wind-swept, layered, and flowing rather than vertical forest silhouettes.

## Horizon read

The horizon should almost never resolve into a familiar terrestrial tree line.

Instead, it should show several depth layers:

- broad low growth close to the ground;
- elevated wind-shaped canopies;
- suspended colonies or large drifting organisms;
- distant forms disappearing into atmospheric haze;
- occasional clear vertical breaks exposing higher cloud layers.

The world should remain recognizable in grayscale through silhouette, atmospheric depth, and horizontal biological structure.

## Ecological logic

Life exploits the dense atmosphere rather than merely tolerating it.

### Ground-adapted life

- low broad organisms anchored across large areas;
- mat-like or radial growth that resists wind shear;
- organisms that collect condensed moisture from air;
- root/anchor systems adapted to soft or waterlogged substrate.

### Elevated and suspended life

- tethered buoyant colonies;
- semi-buoyant reproductive bodies;
- large membrane organisms riding stable wind layers;
- drifting filter-feeders using atmospheric particulates;
- colonial organisms that alter buoyancy to move between layers.

### Fauna

- gliders rather than bird analogues;
- electro-sensitive organisms responding to storm activity;
- predators using fog layers and vertical currents for ambush;
- surface creatures with wide, low profiles where wind exposure is high;
- organisms that shelter in pressure shadows behind geology or vegetation.

The important rule is that form follows pressure, wind, moisture, visibility, and buoyancy.

## Biological visual restraint

Pelagos should not become a catalogue of unrelated alien organisms.

A limited number of recurring structural motifs should connect the biosphere:

- broad radial fans;
- stretched membranes;
- flexible ribs;
- porous filter surfaces;
- tension cables/filaments grown biologically;
- aerodynamic curvature.

This creates the impression of evolutionary families rather than procedural asset variety.

## Palette and light

Diffuse illumination is the baseline.

Characteristics:

- soft shadows;
- strong aerial perspective;
- muted distant color;
- local contrast emerging suddenly at close range;
- occasional hard silhouette flashes during electrical storms;
- brighter upper layers visible through breaks in cloud.

Color can be unusual, but it must remain subordinate to form.

No "everything is purple/blue therefore alien" treatment.

Bioluminescence, if present, should have a functional reason such as signaling, predation, mating, or navigation and should become more apparent in low-light layers rather than coating the whole ecosystem.

## Terrain and hydrology

Pelagos should not be completely flat simply because it is wet.

Useful terrain structures:

- broad eroded ridges breaking wind;
- shallow flooded basins;
- saturated plains;
- exposed rock spines;
- cliff bands creating pressure shadows;
- elevated dry shelves;
- storm-cut channels;
- sink basins collecting water and organic material.

These provide readable navigation structure beneath the biological layers.

## Human adaptation

Human structures should visibly respond to the atmosphere and ground conditions.

Settlements use:

- elevated foundations above unstable saturated ground;
- short, heavily braced structural spans;
- aerodynamic or rounded exposed forms;
- storm shutters and recessed exterior equipment;
- substantial lightning protection;
- local weather and wind instrumentation;
- short-range beacons because visual range is unreliable;
- enclosed connectors rather than long exposed walkways;
- sheltered/recessed landing facilities placed in terrain pressure shadows;
- drainage and condensation management as visible infrastructure.

A generic Starfield prefab dropped into a swamp is a failure state.

## Ship-scale gameplay

Pelagos should make atmospheric flight mechanically and visually different from Khepri.

Potential experiences:

- instrument-assisted navigation through fog;
- flying below one biological canopy and above another;
- sudden emergence into clear atmospheric pockets;
- threading around large suspended colonies;
- navigating wind-sheltered valleys and cliff corridors;
- avoiding or entering storm cells;
- locating settlements by beacon before line-of-sight contact;
- sensor contacts appearing long before visual identification;
- vertical movement between ground haze, biological layers, and clearer upper atmosphere.

The terrain and ecology must leave intentional ship-scale corridors. A beautiful ecosystem that makes flight impossible everywhere would undermine one of the project's central pillars.

## Signature location — The Lantern Chain

An atmospheric research network spans several elevated rock shelves and engineered platforms along a major wind corridor.

Each station carries a high-output navigation beacon. In poor conditions only one or two beacons are visible at a time, forming a sequential approach route through fog and suspended life.

A lower abandoned platform has been repurposed by smugglers because it sits beneath the normal traffic layer and inside a persistent radar/visibility shadow.

Approach sequence:

```text
upper haze
-> beacon acquisition
-> descend through cloud break
-> first suspended colonies
-> low-visibility corridor
-> storm-shadow ridge
-> Lantern Chain platform
-> hidden lower smuggler station
```

## Alienness target

Pelagos should feel alive everywhere without reading as a rainforest, swamp, kelp forest, or cloud forest transplanted wholesale from Earth.

Real biological and atmospheric references are inputs for structure, not templates to copy.

## Hard visual rules

- no terrestrial forest horizon as the dominant silhouette;
- no giant-mushroom shorthand;
- no uniform emissive vegetation;
- no arbitrary floating rocks;
- suspended life must have an ecological/buoyancy rationale;
- vegetation and fauna share recurring structural motifs;
- atmospheric depth is a core visual tool, not merely fog added afterward;
- human architecture visibly responds to wind, moisture, lightning, soft ground, and poor visibility;
- usable ship corridors must be designed into the environment.

## Failure mode to avoid

If Pelagos can be described accurately as "a foggy Starfield forest with alien plants," the concept has failed.
