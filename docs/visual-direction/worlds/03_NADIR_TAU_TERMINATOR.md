# Nadir-Tau — Tidally Locked Terminator World

Status: reference world / climate-structure concept

## Purpose

Nadir-Tau is the fourth benchmark world. Its purpose is to prove that an entire planet can gain identity from one permanent global direction: starward versus nightward.

The world should not merely have a hot biome beside a cold biome. Geography, atmosphere, hydrology, ecology, settlement, architecture, resource use and flight should all respond to the same fixed thermal gradient.

## One-sentence pitch

Nadir-Tau is a relatively dry tidally locked world where limited atmospheric heat redistribution leaves a broad but sharply structured twilight belt between a hostile permanent day side and a frozen permanent night side, forcing life and civilization to grow along the planet rather than across it.

## Physical baseline

Working design assumptions:

- rocky terrestrial planet in synchronous rotation with its host star;
- one hemisphere permanently faces the star and one remains in permanent darkness;
- moderate atmosphere: thick enough to support circulation and weather, but not thick enough to erase the day/night thermal contrast;
- comparatively dry surface, reducing oceanic heat redistribution;
- broad habitable or semi-habitable twilight/terminator belt rather than a mathematically thin line;
- strong persistent circulation carrying heat from starward regions toward the night side;
- large permanent temperature, pressure and illumination gradients across short planetary distances;
- major volatile transport toward colder nightward terrain;
- limited open surface liquid concentrated where pressure and temperature permit it in the twilight belt;
- extensive frost, ice and cold-trapped volatiles farther nightward.

A narrow habitable terminator is a chosen world design, not an automatic consequence of tidal locking. Atmosphere, water inventory and heat transport must remain part of the explanation.

## Stellar and sky behavior

The star remains fixed or nearly fixed in the sky from any given surface location.

This is one of the world's strongest visual cues.

- starward regions experience permanent high or medium stellar elevation depending on longitude;
- the twilight belt sees the star permanently near the horizon;
- nightward regions never see the star directly;
- shadows therefore have a persistent preferred direction;
- there is no ordinary day/night cycle to reset the player's visual expectations.

Local weather may change illumination, but the celestial geometry does not.

## Climate structure

### Dayward furnace

Not necessarily molten or Venus-like, but too hot and exposed for ordinary settlement.

Characteristics:

- intense constant irradiation;
- desiccated and thermally fractured terrain;
- strong convection and cloud formation where atmospheric moisture remains;
- bright haze and severe glare;
- exposed mineral and salt residues where volatiles migrated away.

### Inner twilight

Hot, windy, and intermittently tolerable.

Characteristics:

- powerful star-to-night circulation;
- dry erosion corridors;
- warm rock faces;
- sparse heat-adapted life;
- solar and thermal-energy infrastructure.

### Habitable twilight belt

The primary living corridor.

Characteristics:

- permanent low-angle illumination;
- long shadows;
- strongest concentration of settlements and ecology;
- surface or near-surface liquid possible in locally favorable pressure/temperature zones;
- persistent cross-gradient winds;
- strong atmospheric depth toward both horizons.

### Outer twilight

Cold, dim, and increasingly ice dominated.

Characteristics:

- frost fields;
- freezing runoff and buried ice;
- cold-adapted organisms;
- geothermal and industrial habitation;
- strong temperature inversions and fog in low terrain.

### Permanent night

Not necessarily geologically dead.

Characteristics:

- extreme cold at the surface;
- permanent darkness except aurora, lightning, artificial light and reflected atmospheric glow;
- large cold traps for volatiles;
- glaciers or deep ice deposits where inventory permits;
- geothermal oases, volcanic zones or chemically powered ecosystems as rare exceptions.

## Fluids and volatile transport

Water remains the default working volatile unless an in-game host planet suggests another chemistry.

The important rule is that water distribution is asymmetric.

Potential behavior:

- dayward surface water is scarce because heating and atmospheric transport remove it;
- vapor is transported toward cooler longitudes;
- precipitation and condensation become more likely through the twilight band;
- rivers or runoff trend preferentially toward colder terrain where topography permits;
- outer-twilight channels progressively freeze;
- large reservoirs can become trapped as night-side ice;
- geothermal pockets can maintain isolated liquid beneath or within the frozen hemisphere.

Do not create arbitrary oceans crossing all climate zones unchanged.

## Geological and erosion logic

Tidal locking does not itself carve every ridge parallel to the terminator. The terrain should combine ordinary tectonic/impact/volcanic history with climate-driven modification.

Climate effects can reinforce a global directional bias through:

- persistent wind erosion;
- thermal expansion and contraction gradients;
- freeze/thaw and frost wedging near the cold boundary;
- volatile deposition toward night;
- chemical weathering concentrated in the temperate belt;
- sediment transport aligned with prevailing circulation and drainage.

The underlying geology may cross the climate bands, while surface modification reveals the permanent gradient.

## Shape language

Primary form: long ridges, shelves and escarpments whose weathered faces differ sharply between starward and nightward sides.

Secondary form: climate-band terraces, frost fronts, dry erosion corridors and ice-filled valleys.

Detail form: thermal cracking, directional abrasion, mineral/salt crusts, frost laminations and wind-shaped growth.

Void form: deep lee valleys, shadowed faults, geothermal caverns and ice caves.

Directional bias: strong starward/nightward asymmetry rather than perfectly parallel procedural stripes.

## Horizon read

A single panorama should contain two different futures.

Facing starward:

- fixed brilliant star near the horizon;
- amber/white glare;
- dry bright terrain;
- haze and rising atmosphere;
- harsher exposed silhouettes.

Facing nightward:

- blue-black or neutral darkness;
- ice/frost reflections;
- atmospheric glow or aurora where appropriate;
- colder fog in depressions;
- settlement lights visible at great distance.

The player should learn direction from the world itself without opening a map.

## Ecology

Life is concentrated along the climate corridor and organized around a permanent energy gradient.

### Flora / sessile life

- asymmetric growth with light-harvesting surfaces aimed starward;
- protected roots and reproductive structures on nightward sides;
- low wind-resistant silhouettes;
- dark or reflective thermal-control surfaces depending on niche;
- some species spanning several temperature bands through connected underground structures.

### Fauna / mobile life

- migration primarily along the terminator rather than day/night seasonal migration;
- grazing/predation routes follow stable climate contours;
- excursions starward or nightward are specialized behaviors;
- burrowing and geothermal refuge become increasingly important toward darkness.

### Night-side exceptions

Rare ecosystems may exploit:

- geothermal heat;
- volcanism;
- chemical gradients;
- subsurface liquid reservoirs.

They should feel exceptional and locally motivated, not like normal surface forests placed in darkness.

## Human adaptation

Settlement form follows the permanent gradient.

Communities tend to extend linearly along favorable climate contours rather than expand evenly in all directions.

Architecture may use:

- highly reflective/insulated starward surfaces;
- radiator and heat-rejection infrastructure oriented nightward;
- wind barriers aligned across persistent circulation;
- buried service corridors;
- solar collectors and thermal industry toward the bright edge;
- cold-storage, computation, cryogenic industry and radiators toward the dark edge;
- water/ice extraction infrastructure extending nightward;
- transit routes running parallel to the terminator because those preserve climate conditions.

The architecture should physically reveal which side is starward even when the star is hidden by weather.

## Ship-scale gameplay

Nadir-Tau should turn direction into a strategic choice.

Potential flight experiences:

- low-level flight along the twilight corridor under a fixed horizon star;
- starward excursions into increasing glare, heat and turbulence;
- nightward runs into rapidly worsening darkness, icing/frost and sensor dependence;
- following linear settlements and infrastructure for navigation;
- crossing climate bands quickly in a ship and visibly watching the environment transform;
- using permanent star direction as an orientation cue;
- approaching facilities from their thermal or sheltered side;
- finding concealed night-side entrances visible mainly through lights/sensors.

The ideal experience is that a ten-minute flight perpendicular to the terminator feels like travelling between different planetary worlds while still remaining geographically continuous.

## Weather

Weather should also have a directional logic.

Candidate systems:

- persistent star-to-night near-surface winds;
- major cloud decks forming toward the warm side where convection is strongest;
- fog and low cloud where transported moisture condenses toward cooler longitudes;
- snow/ice-particle events toward the outer twilight;
- powerful boundary storms where warm and cold air masses interact;
- relatively stable local weather corridors separated by more violent transition zones.

Weather should not wander randomly across climate bands without regard for the permanent heat source.

## Signature location — Meridian Spine

Meridian Spine is a long inhabited ridge running roughly parallel to the terminator through one of the most stable climate bands.

Its two faces perform visibly different jobs.

### Starward face

- power collectors;
- observatories;
- heat-resistant industrial equipment;
- weather instrumentation;
- protected high-energy landing infrastructure.

### Nightward face

- radiator farms;
- cold storage;
- cryogenic processing;
- water/ice handling;
- darker recessed hangars;
- concealed access tunnels.

An abandoned subsurface transit system follows the ridge for many kilometres. A hostile group occupies sections of it and uses the dark-side entrances for concealed ship operations.

## Visual benchmark scene — Meridian Crossing

The first concept should place a ship above or alongside Meridian Spine with the permanent star low on one horizon.

One half of the landscape trends toward hot, bright, desiccated terrain; the other toward deep blue-black frozen valleys.

The central twilight corridor contains:

- low asymmetric alien life;
- a linear Starfield-like settlement built into the ridge;
- warm industrial light mixed with cold nightward lighting;
- atmosphere streaming laterally across the scene;
- visible ice beginning on the dark side;
- no conventional sunrise/sunset ambiguity: this lighting state is permanent.

The image must communicate the global gradient without requiring explanatory text.

## Alienness target

Nadir-Tau should feel alien because normal planetary intuitions fail:

- the sun does not move;
- climate has a permanent compass direction;
- settlements grow along temperature contours;
- water migrates toward darkness;
- ecology is asymmetric;
- crossing longitude is more environmentally significant than travelling the same distance along the terminator.

## Hard visual rules

- no ordinary day/night cycle as the primary presentation;
- no simple orange-biome/blue-biome split;
- no razor-straight procedural climate boundary;
- underlying geology must continue through the gradient;
- climate modifies geology rather than replacing it;
- liquid distribution must reflect pressure, temperature and volatile transport;
- settlement architecture has distinct starward and nightward faces;
- ecology shows permanent directional adaptation;
- the planet remains recognizable in grayscale through light direction, ice distribution, silhouette and atmospheric depth.

## Implementation unknowns to investigate later

- whether Starfield's planet/day-night system can represent a truly fixed stellar position;
- whether a world can have longitude-dependent climate/weather states at useful scale;
- whether weather and biome selection can follow a continuous gradient rather than coarse region boundaries;
- how surface liquid/ice placement can be constrained by climate bands;
- whether lighting/exposure can transition continuously across large surface distances;
- how procedural POI selection can remain aligned with starward/nightward environmental roles;
- how navigation and compass systems expose or infer the permanent stellar direction.

## Failure mode to avoid

If Nadir-Tau can be described as "a hot zone next to a snow zone," the concept has failed. The entire world must behave as one permanent thermodynamic gradient.
