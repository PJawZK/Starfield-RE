# Eden-9 — Rare Earth-Like Biosphere

Status: reference world / familiarity control case

## Purpose

Eden-9 exists to define what happens when a world really is comparatively familiar.

The project does not reject Earth-like environments. It rejects their use as the default visual solution for life-bearing planets. Eden-9 should feel special partly because the player has already seen worlds such as Khepri, Pelagos, Vitria, Nadir-Tau and Thalassa.

The target reaction is not "this is another forest planet." It is closer to:

> After all that strangeness, this place feels uncannily like home.

## One-sentence pitch

Eden-9 is a rare temperate world with stable liquid water, breathable air, mature mixed ecosystems and broad glacially carved valleys, where humans can build openly enough that the landscape reads as a place to live rather than merely survive.

## Physical baseline

Working assumptions:

- broadly Earth-like surface pressure;
- broadly Earth-like gravity;
- nitrogen/oxygen-dominated breathable atmosphere;
- stable liquid-water cycle;
- temperate climate with meaningful regional and seasonal variation;
- familiar day/night rhythm;
- active hydrology and erosion;
- plate tectonic or equivalent long-term crustal recycling;
- old, biologically mature land surfaces rather than uniformly young volcanic terrain;
- no exotic global atmospheric effect required for visual identity.

The point is not to make a perfect Earth clone. The point is to create a planet where ordinary human intuitions about weather, water, exposure and outdoor movement mostly work.

## Fluids and phase state

Water is the dominant surface fluid.

Expected surface phenomena include:

- rivers;
- lakes;
- wetlands;
- snowpack and alpine ice;
- rain;
- mist;
- seasonal floodplains;
- groundwater-fed springs;
- glacially sourced braided rivers in high-relief regions.

Because the environment is benign, these features should be visually common enough to contrast with harsher worlds where stable surface water is absent or highly constrained.

## Geological identity

Eden-9 should remain visually specific despite its familiarity.

The benchmark region is a broad glacially carved valley system with:

- steep mountain walls;
- braided or meandering river channels;
- gravel bars and floodplains;
- forested lower slopes;
- open meadow/wetland transitions;
- alpine basins and snow-fed tributaries;
- occasional cliffs and river gorges;
- layered terrain revealing long erosion history.

This is deliberately closer to real temperate mountain environments than the other reference worlds.

The distinction from a generic Earth scan comes from recombination: the river geometry, vegetation families, mountain structure, weather pattern and settlement history should belong to one coherent planet rather than copy a recognizable real-world national park.

## Shape language

Primary form: broad river-cut and glacial valleys enclosed by mountain systems.

Secondary form: forested ridges, wetlands, cliffs, open meadow shelves and braided floodplains.

Detail form: recognizable soils, rounded river stone, leaf-like and needle-like structures, bark-like tissues, flowers/spore bodies, moss-like ground cover and flowing water.

Void form: caves, river gorges, forest clearings, alpine basins and sheltered valleys.

Directional bias: comparatively weak and varied. This is intentional: mature erosion and ecology have produced a less aggressively singular shape language than Vitria or Nadir-Tau.

## Horizon read

Eden-9 is allowed to trigger familiarity immediately.

Typical horizon structure:

- forested lower elevations;
- broad valley floor;
- visible river system;
- rocky upper slopes;
- distant alpine snow or cloud-wrapped peaks.

It should remain visually convincing in neutral overcast conditions and should not depend on golden-hour cinematography to look attractive.

## Ecology

The biosphere is complex and mature, with ecological roles that feel intuitively understandable.

Possible recurring structures:

- tall photosynthetic canopy organisms;
- understory shrubs and broad low ground cover;
- meadow/prairie analogues;
- wetland vegetation;
- pollinator analogues;
- grazers;
- browsers;
- predators;
- aquatic food webs;
- seasonal migration and reproductive cycles.

Species do not need extreme morphology simply to prove they are extraterrestrial.

However, they should still belong to a small number of coherent evolutionary families rather than be exact copies of Earth species.

Examples of subtle alien cues:

- leaf arrangements unfamiliar but functionally recognizable;
- unusual branching ratios;
- slightly different canopy geometry;
- different reproductive bodies;
- fauna locomotion/body plans that remain believable without becoming grotesque.

The biological goal is convergent familiarity rather than hidden Earth imports.

## Weather and atmosphere

Weather should be readable and naturalistic:

- clear days;
- layered cloud;
- rain systems;
- valley fog;
- snow at altitude;
- thunderstorms;
- seasonal frontal weather.

Weather does not need to be dangerous every time it appears.

This itself is important contrast: on Eden-9, rain can simply be rain.

## Human adaptation

Because the environment is relatively benign, humans can build in ways that are uncommon elsewhere in the project.

Settlements can include:

- open streets and courtyards;
- agriculture;
- bridges and road networks;
- unpressurized outdoor work areas;
- larger windows and exposed public spaces;
- civilian recreation;
- riverside industry;
- open landing fields where terrain allows;
- farms and distributed rural structures rather than only compact survival compounds.

The architecture should still retain Starfield's grounded human engineering language, but survival hardware is no longer the dominant visual story.

This provides a strong contrast with worlds where almost everything is buried, shielded, recessed or storm-protected.

## Settlement ecology

Eden-9 should also show ecological restraint from humans rather than unrestricted suburban sprawl.

A desirable visual balance is:

- one substantial valley settlement;
- agricultural clearings;
- small satellite communities;
- utility and transport corridors;
- very large uninterrupted wilderness areas beyond them.

That preserves exploration and makes the world feel large.

## Ship-scale gameplay

Flight here should feel liberating rather than adversarial.

Useful routes include:

- river-valley following;
- mountain passes;
- coastal cliffs;
- forested basins;
- canyon approaches;
- alpine lakes;
- agricultural valleys;
- remote mountain settlements;
- natural landing clearings and prepared pads.

Unlike Vitria, the environment is not constantly trying to knock the ship out of the sky. Unlike Pelagos, visibility is generally good. The appeal is freedom, scale and landscape reading.

That contrast matters for pacing across the larger game.

## Signature location — First Valley

First Valley is a broad river basin settled early because it combines:

- breathable air;
- abundant freshwater;
- deep soil;
- manageable terrain;
- hydropower potential;
- easy ship approaches;
- mountain shelter;
- access to mineral-rich uplands.

The valley contains:

- a long-established civilian settlement;
- farms and greenhouses integrated into open fields;
- river bridges and water infrastructure;
- an older exploration/science base preserved near the original landing site;
- small industrial yards;
- an airfield/landing complex;
- trails and roads extending into the mountains.

Beyond the settlement, a much larger wilderness begins quickly.

A remote alpine region contains old survey installations, caves and hidden locations, providing exploration without requiring the familiar world to become artificially hostile.

## Visual benchmark scene

The first concept image should show:

- a Starfield-like ship flying into a broad inhabited valley;
- a braided or gently meandering river as the main compositional spine;
- mixed forest and meadow rather than uniform tree coverage;
- substantial mountains in the background;
- snow or cloud on higher elevations;
- a civilian settlement integrated with the river and terrain;
- agricultural clearings visible but not dominating the valley;
- open sky and good visibility;
- no fantasy color grading;
- no alien spectacle inserted merely to make the scene look less familiar.

The image should feel unusually calm compared with the other benchmark worlds.

## Narrative and emotional role

Eden-9 is not valuable because it is exotic.

It is valuable because it creates relief.

After repeated exposure to worlds where:

- the terrain is anomalously deformed;
- visibility is poor;
- storms reshape travel;
- ocean geology constrains settlement;
- climate has a permanent global direction;

Eden-9 should feel almost luxurious.

That creates narrative possibilities around migration, land ownership, conservation, colonial pressure, agriculture and political value that would not carry the same weight if breathable garden worlds were common.

## Rarity rule

The project should treat worlds this comfortable as rare.

Familiar individual biomes may appear elsewhere, but the full combination of:

- breathable atmosphere;
- comfortable pressure/gravity;
- stable surface water;
- mild climate;
- mature land biosphere;
- broad agricultural potential;
- low environmental hazard

should be uncommon enough that civilization reacts to such planets accordingly.

## Alienness target

Low by design.

Distinctiveness comes from world geography, ecological coherence, subtle biological differences and human history rather than forced exoticism.

## Hard visual rules

- do not recolor vegetation solely to prove the planet is extraterrestrial;
- do not insert giant mushrooms, floating rocks or glowing flora without ecological reason;
- do not make every pleasant planet an Eden-9 equivalent;
- do not copy a single recognizable Earth location wholesale;
- keep large wilderness areas despite the planet's desirability;
- human settlement should look unusually open and comfortable compared with other benchmark worlds;
- the environment must still have regional variation and history rather than one endless forest biome.

## Failure mode to avoid

If Eden-9 feels generic because "Earth-like" was treated as permission to stop designing, the concept has failed.

Its familiarity must be deliberate, coherent and rare enough to become emotionally meaningful.
