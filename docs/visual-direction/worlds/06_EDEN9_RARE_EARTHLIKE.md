# Eden-9 — Rare Earth-Like Biosphere

Status: reference world / familiarity control case

## Purpose

Eden-9 exists to define what happens when a world really is comparatively familiar.

The project does not reject Earth-like environments. It rejects their use as the default visual solution for life-bearing planets. Eden-9 should feel special partly because the player has seen many stranger worlds first.

## Core physical rules

- broadly Earth-like pressure and temperature range;
- stable liquid-water cycle;
- oxygen-rich atmosphere;
- moderate gravity;
- familiar day/night rhythm;
- mature, complex biosphere.

## Shape language

Primary form: broad river-cut valleys and mountain systems that can plausibly feel familiar.

Secondary form: layered forest canopies, wetlands, cliffs, and open meadows.

Detail form: recognizable soil, leaf-like structures, bark-like surfaces, flowers/spore bodies, flowing water.

Void form: caves, river gorges, forest clearings, alpine basins.

Directional bias: comparatively weak and varied, reinforcing the idea of a mature, diverse biosphere.

## Horizon read

Unlike the other reference worlds, Eden-9 is allowed to trigger familiarity. The player should plausibly think that parts of it resemble Earth.

The key is restraint: it should not look like a direct scan of a specific terrestrial national park. Familiar systems are recombined into their own geography and ecology.

## Ecology

The biosphere can contain recognizable ecological roles:

- tall photosynthetic canopy organisms;
- understory growth;
- pollinator analogues;
- grazers and predators;
- wetlands and aquatic food webs.

Species morphology does not need to be bizarre. The interest is in observing convergent evolution and asking why this world feels unusually comfortable.

## Palette and light

Natural greens/blues/browns are allowed. The project should not recolor familiar biology merely to prove it is alien.

Weather and light should feel physically grounded and inviting compared with most reference worlds.

## Human adaptation

Because the environment is relatively benign, human settlements can expand outward more openly:

- larger exterior spaces;
- agriculture;
- less extreme shielding;
- roads and open landing fields;
- recreation and long-term civilian settlement.

This provides visual contrast with survival-driven installations elsewhere.

## Ship-scale gameplay

Even a familiar world should benefit from planetary flight:

- mountain valleys;
- river canyons;
- coastal cliffs;
- forested basins;
- isolated settlements;
- natural landing clearings.

Flight here should feel liberating rather than hostile.

## Signature location — First Valley

A broad habitable valley contains a long-established civilian settlement, farms, river infrastructure, and an old exploration base. The environment demonstrates what humans build when they do not spend most resources merely surviving the planet.

A remote mountain system beyond the settled valley provides contrasting wilderness and hidden exploration locations.

## Narrative/visual role

Eden-9 should generate a reaction closer to:

> "This place actually feels like home."

That reaction only matters if most other inhabited worlds do not.

## Alienness target

Low by design. Distinctiveness comes from world geography and ecology rather than forced exoticism.

## Failure mode to avoid

Do not make every pleasant life-bearing planet resemble Eden-9. Its familiarity is valuable precisely because it is uncommon.
