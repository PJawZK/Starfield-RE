# Vitria — Silica Storm World

Status: reference world / geology-weather concept

## Purpose

Vitria is the third visual pillar after Khepri and Pelagos. Its job is to prove that a world can feel genuinely alien through chemistry, atmosphere and geology alone, without depending on abundant life or anomalous Artifact effects.

It should look as though the atmosphere has been sculpting the planet in one dominant direction for geological time.

## Physical baseline

Vitria is a dry, mineral-rich terrestrial planet with a substantial but hostile atmosphere.

Working design assumptions:

- atmospheric pressure high enough for powerful sustained surface winds;
- very low available surface water;
- atmosphere carries abundant fine silicate/mineral particulates;
- persistent regional wind regimes remain directionally stable for long periods;
- frequent charged dust fronts and electrostatic activity;
- thick deposits of weakly cemented fine-grained material alternate with harder mineral layers;
- abrasive particles continually sandblast exposed surfaces;
- temperature and chemistry allow localized mineral deposition/cementation in protected zones;
- sparse life is optional and must remain secondary to the geology.

These are design assumptions to be reconciled with whichever in-game planet/environment eventually hosts the prototype.

## Real physical analogue

The principal analogue is yardang terrain on Earth and Mars.

Long-lived wind erosion can carve soft or granular bedrock into aligned ridge-and-trough systems. The orientation records the prevailing wind direction over long periods. Windblown particles act as abrasive material, while differences in hardness and cementation determine which layers remain standing.

Vitria extrapolates this process to much larger and more visually dramatic scales rather than inventing randomly distributed crystal formations.

## Geological history model

1. volcanic or sedimentary processes create thick layers of fine silicate-rich material;
2. burial/chemical processes partially cement those deposits;
3. harder mineral bands form within or beneath them;
4. long-lived atmospheric circulation begins preferential erosion;
5. soft layers are stripped away into parallel corridors;
6. hard layers remain as kilometer-scale fins, ribs and overhangs;
7. abrasive storms polish windward faces and excavate lee-side cavities;
8. finer material redeposits in pressure shadows and sheltered basins.

The result should look intentional because the same atmospheric process shaped everything.

## Shape language

Primary form: long parallel yardang-like mineral ribs aligned with prevailing wind.

Secondary form: leaning blade formations, asymmetric fins and undercut ridges.

Detail form: polished windward facets, laminated surfaces, scalloped abrasion marks, fractured translucent/mineral-rich edges.

Void form: wind slots, lee-side hollows, natural tunnels, protected channels and deep shadow pockets.

Directional bias: extremely strong. A distant landscape should reveal the historic wind direction at a glance.

## Horizon read

The horizon should look combed by the atmosphere.

Instead of ordinary mountain ranges, repeated elongated fins and ridges sweep toward the same vanishing direction. Occasional harder formations interrupt the pattern like islands in a frozen current.

Even without a storm, Vitria should be identifiable from silhouette alone.

## Surface material behavior

Avoid the visual shorthand of loose giant crystals.

Use mineralogy more structurally:

- thin reflective seams inside layered rock;
- translucent edges where erosion exposes purer material;
- dark polished windward surfaces;
- pale particulate deposits in lee zones;
- smooth abrasion facets beside freshly fractured rough surfaces;
- occasional electrically active dust films during storms.

Any apparent liquid-like streak must instead read as solid mineral deposition, dust accumulation, melt/fusion associated with localized heat, or another physically justified process.

## Weather

Storms are not merely visual filters. They are one of the forces that made the world.

A storm front should appear as an advancing wall of particulate atmosphere with embedded electrical discharge, rapidly changing visibility and surface contrast.

Wind direction matters for:

- shelter;
- navigation;
- landing approach;
- exposure hazards;
- visibility;
- exterior equipment placement.

## Ecology

Biology is optional and sparse.

If present, life should exploit pressure shadows and mineral chemistry rather than populate open plains like terrestrial vegetation.

Possible forms:

- mineral-feeding films within protected seams;
- dormant mats beneath overhangs;
- low crust-like colonies in lee pockets;
- organisms exposed only after storms uncover protected surfaces.

Vitria must remain visually successful with all biology removed.

## Human adaptation

Human structures visibly obey the same wind regime.

Facilities use:

- natural fins as windbreaks;
- deeply recessed hangars on lee faces;
- buried service corridors;
- low-profile sacrificial exterior structures;
- abrasion-resistant outer plating;
- protected intakes and radiator geometry;
- sensor masts placed only where exposure is worth the damage;
- landing corridors aligned with local terrain and prevailing wind.

A base placed openly on a flat pad with no environmental adaptation is a design failure.

## Ship-scale gameplay

Directional geology naturally generates readable flight routes:

- high-speed runs along kilometer-scale troughs;
- slalom routes between fins;
- sheltered passages beneath undercut ridges;
- dangerous crossings above exposed ridge crests;
- lee-side landing approaches during storms;
- hidden facilities visible only from the protected side;
- terrain navigation remaining possible when sensors degrade.

The world should visually invite the player to follow the terrain.

## Signature location — Glass Maw

Glass Maw is a huge wind-excavated slot between two exceptionally resistant mineral fins.

A mining and processing installation occupies the protected lee interior. Surface structures are expendable and heavily abraded; the valuable complex, hangars and habitation are buried deeply into the rock.

The main ship entrance cannot be approached safely head-on during severe weather. The normal route follows a long protected trough before turning sharply into the slot.

This makes geology, weather, architecture and flight part of the same encounter.

## Visual benchmark scene

The first concept image should show:

- a Starfield-like ship flying low along the lee side of a giant mineral ridge;
- many kilometer-scale parallel fins receding toward the horizon;
- clearly asymmetric windward and lee faces;
- a dust wall advancing in the distance;
- mineral seams catching diffuse light without becoming a crystal forest;
- a small recessed industrial facility hidden within one sheltered slot;
- strong directional composition so the wind history is obvious before weather effects are considered.

## Alienness target

A viewer should not need unusual vegetation, glowing crystals or Artifact effects to identify Vitria as alien.

The desired reaction is:

> This looks like a process I understand, operating on materials and at a scale I have never seen before.

## Failure modes to avoid

- generic giant-crystal biome;
- randomly oriented rock spires;
- fantasy glass mountains with no erosion logic;
- permanent glowing surfaces as the primary identity;
- fluids where pressure/temperature/chemistry do not support them;
- storm effects used to conceal otherwise generic terrain;
- human structures ignoring the wind regime.
