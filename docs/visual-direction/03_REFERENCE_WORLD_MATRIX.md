# Reference World Comparison Matrix

Status: benchmark comparison baseline

The six reference worlds exist to stress-test the visual language. They should not converge toward one generic definition of "alien," and they should not differ only through color grading or weather presets.

## Comparative identity matrix

| World | Primary identity | Dominant silhouette | Atmosphere / climate | Life | Human architectural expression | Ship-scale hook | Must remain unique |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Khepri | impact geology + localized anomaly | crater rings, terraces, branching collapse trenches | effectively airless | absent | buried pressure networks, crater-wall infrastructure, hidden hangars | crater dives, trench runs, void ingress | monumental emptiness + anomaly-deformed geology |
| Pelagos | dense-atmosphere ecology | horizontal canopies, suspended colonies, layered haze | humid, dense, windy, electrical | abundant | elevated/braced structures integrated with wind, moisture and beacon systems | instrument flight through fog and biological layers | non-terrestrial ecosystem structure |
| Nadir-Tau | permanent climate geometry | long lateral ridges and climate bands | fixed day/night gradient, strong circulation | concentrated in twilight belt | settlement-as-thermal-machine along terminator | cross hot/twilight/cold zones in one flight | directional planetary climate |
| Vitria | chemistry + wind erosion + storms | aligned fins, ribs and lee hollows | abrasive particulate atmosphere, violent fronts | sparse/optional | lee-side, recessed, sacrificial and erosion-aware infrastructure | storm shelter routes and wind-cut corridors | world-scale directional erosion |
| Thalassa | ocean + vertical volcanic coast | sea stacks, caldera walls, arches, vertical spines | humid, maritime, storm-prone | mainly marine/coastal | cliff-integrated, floating and amphibious systems | sea arches, calderas, platform approaches | vertical civilization around scarce stable land |
| Eden-9 | rare familiar biosphere | river valleys, mountains, mature canopy | mild, breathable, familiar weather | abundant | ecologically integrated mature interstellar settlement | broad free flight through habitable terrain | deliberate familiarity + future-human civic maturity |

## Distinction test

Each concept should remain distinguishable when reduced to:

- grayscale;
- one horizon silhouette;
- one ship-approach screenshot;
- one ground-level screenshot;
- one POI exterior;
- one settlement/infrastructure crop with most of the environment hidden.

If two worlds become visually interchangeable under those conditions, their structural rules need revision.

## Shared project-wide language

Some things may repeat across worlds because they belong to the civilization, technology base, or game rather than to one planet.

Safe to repeat with adaptation:

- standardized docking interfaces;
- pressure/airlock logic;
- recognizable human ergonomics;
- modular cargo standards;
- common ship families;
- signage and navigation conventions;
- faction-level materials and manufacturing signatures;
- emergency lighting conventions;
- maintenance access logic;
- recurring corporate/faction branding;
- broad engineering ancestry from Starfield's NASA-punk language;
- ecological stewardship as a mature-civilization principle where local conditions permit it.

These shared elements should create civilizational continuity, but their deployment must adapt to local conditions.

## Elements that must not repeat unchanged

The following should be world-conditioned rather than universal prefab decisions:

- settlement footprint;
- landing approach geometry;
- exterior structural envelope;
- foundation strategy;
- cooling/heating expression;
- weather protection;
- water handling;
- vegetation/ecology relationship;
- dominant transport pattern;
- visibility/navigation aids;
- exterior maintenance strategy;
- hazard mitigation;
- POI placement logic;
- horizon composition around major settlements.

A settlement that could be moved unchanged from one benchmark world to another has failed.

## Coverage provided by the six concepts

The set intentionally spans:

- no atmosphere -> very dense atmosphere;
- sterile -> highly biological;
- hostile -> comfortable;
- vertical -> horizontal dominant forms;
- open visibility -> severe visibility limits;
- geology-driven -> ecology-driven identity;
- static climate -> moving storm hazard -> permanent directional climate;
- sparse human presence -> mature civilian settlement;
- survival-driven architecture -> hopeful ecological integration;
- low-altitude precision routes -> broad exploratory flight.

## Benchmark contrast pairs

Useful pairs for future reviews:

- Khepri vs Eden-9: hostile emptiness vs comfortable abundance.
- Pelagos vs Eden-9: alien living structure vs familiar living structure.
- Vitria vs Thalassa: dry directional erosion vs fluid vertical coast.
- Nadir-Tau vs Vitria: permanent global directionality vs transient weather-driven directionality.
- Khepri vs Vitria: barren geology from impact/collapse vs barren geology from atmospheric erosion.
- Pelagos vs Thalassa: atmosphere-dominated navigation vs ocean/coast-dominated navigation.

If these pairs begin to feel interchangeable, procedural reuse has gone too far.

## What is not yet represented

Future concepts may explore:

- true cryogenic chemistry worlds;
- subsurface-primary biospheres;
- megastructure/ruin-dominated worlds;
- very high-gravity environments;
- low-density super-Earth atmospheres;
- ring worlds / artificial habitats if technically and thematically appropriate;
- highly volcanic or magma-dominated environments;
- planet-scale biological structures;
- worlds where the primary exploration layer is underground.

These should be added only if they introduce genuinely new structural rules rather than filling a checklist.

## Benchmark status

All six reference worlds now have first-pass visual benchmarks. The next phase is to use them as constraints for procedural identity rather than continuing to add more concepts.
