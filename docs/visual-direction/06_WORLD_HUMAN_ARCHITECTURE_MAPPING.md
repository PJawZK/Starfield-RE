# Human Architectural Expression Across the Six Benchmark Worlds

Status: project-wide application of `05_HUMAN_FUTURE_ARCHITECTURE.md`

This document applies the human-future architecture doctrine to the six benchmark worlds. The goal is to ensure that human settlements remain culturally and technologically unfamiliar while still making engineering sense and visibly cooperating with local planetary systems.

## Khepri — anomaly-scarred crater moon

Human architecture should merge with crater geology rather than sit on top of it.

- pressure-capable habitation is embedded into crater walls and stable shelves;
- hangars are excavated into fracture corridors and natural voids;
- structural ribs tie directly into competent rock instead of relying on large exposed pads;
- power, thermal storage, shielding and cargo movement share buried corridors;
- exposed elements are sparse and intentionally sacrificial;
- sensor and defense structures occupy rim positions only where the line-of-sight benefit justifies exposure;
- anomaly research infrastructure should look temporary or heavily isolated from permanent habitation.

Ecological integration here means geological integration: humanity uses the moon's shielding, voids, stable strata and thermal environment rather than brute-forcing an exposed surface settlement.

## Pelagos — dense-atmosphere float biosphere

Architecture should become part of the atmospheric ecosystem rather than interrupt it.

- settlements occupy stable rock shelves and aerodynamic pressure shadows;
- structural shells are rounded, ribbed or membrane-supported where that improves wind loading;
- suspended bridges are minimized in favor of enclosed short-span connectors;
- beacon, lightning-protection and atmospheric-sensing systems are integrated into structural spines;
- water capture, condensation management and drainage are visible parts of the architecture;
- landing facilities occupy calm flow zones rather than arbitrary flat platforms;
- ecological corridors pass beneath, through and around settlement volumes.

The desired effect is not a city dropped into alien vegetation, but a human habitat that has learned the same atmospheric rules as the native biosphere.

## Vitria — silica storm world

Human architecture should visibly reveal the prevailing wind before the player checks a weather instrument.

- facilities grow along lee faces of mineral fins;
- outer structures use replaceable abrasion skins and recessed service interfaces;
- critical habitation, processing and transit are buried deeply into protected rock;
- landing and freight corridors run with the dominant flow rather than across it;
- ridge-top sensors are minimal, modular and expendable;
- thermal and power infrastructure is integrated into sheltered structural masses;
- storm refuge spaces form part of normal circulation rather than emergency-only rooms.

There should be no conventional open industrial park. The settlement is a machine shaped by erosion and shelter.

## Nadir-Tau — tidally locked terminator world

The settlement should behave like a continuous thermal machine stretched along the habitable gradient.

- starward surfaces collect power and tolerate radiative heat load;
- nightward surfaces reject heat and support cryogenic industry;
- habitation occupies the intermediate thermal band;
- buried transit spines connect functions across the gradient;
- structural orientation follows the terminator rather than conventional radial city growth;
- wind barriers and terrain ridges become part of the settlement envelope;
- agriculture and controlled ecologies occupy thermal niches instead of generic fields.

The architecture should make it obvious that "toward the star" and "toward the night" are different engineering directions.

## Thalassa — oceanic archipelago world

Human settlement should read vertically and amphibiously rather than like a coastal city with futuristic towers.

- cliff anchors carry habitation and industry above storm surge;
- caldera walls form natural structural shells and harbors;
- floating infrastructure is tied into anchored cliff systems rather than operating as disconnected platforms;
- vertical lifts, submerged service routes and air/sea logistics dominate over roads;
- water processing, tidal power, storm monitoring and marine ecology are integrated into civic systems;
- ship hangars occupy protected cavities and lee-facing cuts;
- ecological marine corridors remain continuous through settled waters.

The ocean is not scenery around the settlement. It is part of the transport, energy, climate and ecological infrastructure.

## Eden-9 — rare Earth-like biosphere

Eden-9 is the strongest expression of hopeful ecological maturity.

- large-scale settlement remains visually advanced and culturally unfamiliar;
- built systems follow watersheds, cliffs and stable terrain instead of flattening valleys;
- ecological corridors remain continuous through inhabited regions;
- agriculture is integrated into the urban fabric as controlled landscape systems rather than isolated rectangular fields;
- passenger, cargo and utility transport are layered, reducing road dominance;
- habitat, civic, power, water and thermal systems merge into multi-function structural complexes;
- pressure-capable or environmentally controllable shells remain common because they are part of a mature interstellar construction language, not because the planet is hostile;
- natural rivers, forests and slopes remain legible as functioning ecosystems rather than decorative greenery between buildings.

Eden-9 should produce the clearest synthesis of the two architecture benchmarks: alien future structuring plus ecological integration.

## Shared rule

Across all six worlds, the settlement should pass the same test:

> The viewer understands what humans are trying to accomplish, but would not have designed the solution this way today.

The environment must remain an active design input. A settlement that could be moved unchanged to another benchmark world has failed.
